📖 Spatial Layout & Audio Guide
🌐 1. What Does the 3D Plane Display?

The 3D visualizer plane displays the actual discrete audio channels currently being fed into the 3D spatial panner.

Each speaker sphere in 3D space represents a dedicated Web Audio PannerNode placed at exact ITU-R coordinate positions (azimuth, elevation, and distance) relative to your head. As you turn your head wearing the Sony motion sensor, the binaural HRTF rotates the soundfield in real time so sounds stay anchored in 3D space.

🎧 2. Why Does a Dolby AC-4 File Show 5.1 When 7.1 Is Selected?

There is a fundamental difference between what the AC-4 standard allows vs. how your specific file was encoded:

  • The AC-4 Standard: Supports 1.0 Mono, 2.0 Stereo, 5.1 Surround, 7.1.4 Immersive Beds, up to dynamic 3D objects.
  • Your Audio File: Most broadcast and streaming AC-4 files are mastered and broadcasted as 5.1 Surround presentations (6 physical channels: FL, FR, Center, LFE, Surround Left, Surround Right).

Because the file contains 6 discrete channels of audio data, selecting 7.1 in the player will not invent 2 non-existent rear speakers (which would be silent). The player faithfully renders the true 5.1 spatial field mixed by the sound engineer.

📻 3. What Happens When Selecting 2.0 Stereo?

For AC-4 tracks decoded by ffcodec64.dll, all 6 native channels are extracted so that Center dialog and surround sound are never discarded. The visualizer places all 6 speakers in 3D space around you to give you full spatial immersion. For standard files (FLAC/WAV/MKV), selecting 2.0 downmixes the audio into stereo.

🛰️ 4. MPEG-H VVPlayer & Sony 360 Reality Audio

Fraunhofer MPEG-H VVPlayer is an external player designed to decode proprietary Sony 360 RA and MPEG-H 3D audio files. Use the 🎙️ Hook Live Audio button in the MPEG-H card to capture VVPlayer's audio output into SpatialAudio, bringing 3D visualization and head tracking to your MPEG-H tracks!
